01The work

Every job arrives as a work order.

The order names the client, the site, the aircraft and cameras the job needs, and what you hand over at the end. You check the weather window and the airspace, write the risk assessment and brief the crew. Then you fly one or more batteries, process the footage and send it to a client who reacts.

02On site

You carry the aircraft from the case to the pad yourself.

You start at the ute, in first person. Unfold the arms, take the gimbal cover off, walk back to the pilot station and tick the checks on the controller screen. Then the motors spin up and you have control.

03The map

Ten work sites, from the Perth Hills to the Pilbara coast.

Each site is at real world scale, with the plants, light and weather of its region. The seasons change as well. The Wheatbelt goes from green crop to stubble, and the Murchison flowers after winter rain.

Outline from ABS state boundaries, CC BY 4.0. Pins show regions, not exact sites.

04In the air

You fly each kind of job differently.

You frame a house for an agent, cover a paddock for a survey, hunt hot cells across a solar block, or thread a cinewhoop through a doorway while a spotter calls the gaps.

06The company

You start with a borrowed ute and end up with a hangar.

The yard is your company's own block on the Swan Valley fringe, and you walk around it. It grows with what you buy. You trade the sea container store for a shed, then a hangar, and your crew park their cars there. After a bad job, your money and reputation recover. The weather can still upset the plan.

07Academy

Learn the real rules, then fly by them.

Training mode opens the career with lessons, practice quizzes and checkrides built on the real rules in three countries.
